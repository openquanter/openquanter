## Before you read the legal text

Two different things are required from contributors, and they are easy to
confuse:

| | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| **DCO sign-off** | A one-line certification (`Signed-off-by:`) that you wrote the code or otherwise have the right to submit it. Checked automatically by CI. | **Every commit.** |
| **CLA** | A licensing agreement granting the project the rights it needs to distribute your contribution. | **Substantial contributions only**, when a maintainer asks. |

Three things worth knowing before you sign, stated plainly:

1. **You keep your copyright.** This agreement is a licence, not an
   assignment. You remain free to use your own contribution however you like.
2. **The project may relicense.** The licence you grant includes the right to
   sublicense. In practice this means the maintainers can distribute your
   contribution under Apache-2.0 today and could adopt a different licence in
   the future without tracking down every contributor. If that possibility is
   unacceptable to you, do not sign — and say so; a contribution that cannot
   be relicensed can still be discussed, it simply cannot be merged.
3. **Signing creates no obligation to accept your work,** and no obligation on
   you to provide support.

The text below is what actually binds. Sections 1–8 apply to individuals;
section 9 covers contributions made on behalf of an employer.

## How to sign

**Individuals.** When a maintainer requests a CLA on a pull request, add a
comment to that pull request containing:

```
I have read the OpenQuanter Contributor License Agreement (CLA.md)
and I hereby agree to its terms for this and my future contributions.

Full name:      <your legal name>
GitHub username: <your handle>
E-mail:         <the e-mail address used in your commits>
```

The comment, together with the commit history it refers to, is the record of
agreement. You only need to do this once.

**Entities.** Open an issue titled `Corporate CLA: <entity name>` and the
maintainers will respond with the process and the current point of contact.
Do not post employee lists or contact details in a public issue.

Records of agreement are retained by the maintainers for as long as the
contributed code remains in the project.
